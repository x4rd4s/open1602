## What's new in 0.5.0

Changes since [0.4.2](https://github.com/x4rd4s/open1602/releases/tag/v0.4.2): a map and scenario
editor, worlds of islands of their own shape up to 4000 x 4000 tiles, and content packages that
can now bring whole scenarios, campaigns, figures, ships, climates and languages.

Multiplayer games need every player on 0.5.0: the network protocol has changed. Games saved with
earlier versions still load.

### New

- **Editor:** "Editor" in the main menu (or `open1602 --editor`) makes and changes maps and
  scenarios in the game's window, with a menu bar, a tool bar and a status line of its own.
  - Islands: join islands, draw land and water, tidy the coast, paint terrain with a brush of
    several shapes and sizes, fill, replace, copy, turn, mirror and paste pieces, and use island
    templates as stamps.
  - Place buildings for any player (also the natives' and pirates'), ships, land units, the free
    trader's and the pirates' ship, ore and gold veins, treasures and deer; rename cities, fill
    their stock and give them to another player.
  - The whole map: up to twelve players with kind, name, colour, money and allowances, diplomacy at
    the start, the scenario's own game rules, random elements with a dice preview, goals and loss
    conditions per player, a description with stars and introduction, markers and a script.
  - Comforts: undo and redo, straight rows, the reason a placement is refused before the click,
    recently used objects, find islands, cities and ships by name (Ctrl+F), view bookmarks, the
    map saved as a picture, and recovery of unsaved maps.
  - **Check** lists what is wrong with a map and compares the start areas (land, crops, ore,
    gold, nearest rival); a click on a line goes to the place it is about.
  - **Test play** (F5) starts the map as a game and comes back to the editor.
  - Opens the original's scenarios and saved games, and saves scenarios into a content package,
    also as a part of the package's campaign.
- **Islands of their own shape:** a world of its own can now draw every island itself instead of
  using the original's island templates. The new page "Island world" in the start dialog chooses
  the island shapes (original, mixed or own) and, for own shapes, the share of land, the smallest
  and largest island, the spread of sizes, gaps, coast, outline, forest and ore mountains, with
  presets (Varied, Archipelago, Continents, Few giants, Even) and a preview of the world; "Reroll"
  draws another. Own islands have rivers, slopes around the ore mountains, varied ground, lakes,
  volcanoes and native villages, and can be up to 1000 tiles on a side.
- **Larger worlds:** maps up to 4000 x 4000 tiles (2800 x 1960, 4000 x 2800 and 4000 x 4000 in
  the start dialog). A large map gets the land asked for, every start has both climates within
  reach, and large worlds and their preview are made several times faster.
- **Island resources known from the start:** a new setting on the page "Island world". Every player,
  the computers too, knows the fertility and the ore and gold deposits of every island when the
  game starts; their symbols show on islands not yet discovered.
- **Explanations of the game settings:** when the mouse rests on a setting, a row or a preset, a
  tooltip says what it does and how the original game does it.
- **Key settings:** a new window, opened from the window "Display", lists every action with its
  keys. A click waits for the new key, a right click adds a second one, Backspace removes it, and
  "Defaults" brings back Open1602's keys.
- **Houses in their different looks:** placed, dragged-out, planned and copied houses take one of
  their looks each, as in the original. A demolish rectangle that holds only houses takes them all,
  so a block of houses is cleared in one drag.
- **Joining a multiplayer game** shows its progress: the host preparing the game, a bar with the
  megabytes received, and loading. Every lobby has a heading in each player's own language.
- **Improved computer players** (with "Computer players" not set to "As the original"): they
  settle beside another computer's young city on a large island, and a city that lacks a ware
  fetches it by trade cart from a partner's city on the same island.

### Mods

Content packages (folders in the `mods` folder of Open1602's user data folder) can now also bring:

- scenarios with maps, goals, starting money and a script, and campaigns of up to five of them in a
  row, listed in the main menu;
- factions of their own, figures and soldiers of their own, ships for the shipyards, and figures
  and ships drawn with their own images in every player colour;
- climates, island templates and kinds of island world of their own;
- the computer players' build orders, producers and stage values as data, so the computers build
  the package's production chains;
- replacements for the original's art, sounds, music, message voices, coats of arms and flags
  (PNG), icons up to 1024 pixels, the interface's art and videos in WebM (AV1 and Opus);
- languages (`locale/<code>.lang`);
- scripts that hear about cities, disasters, ships, fights and diplomacy, and that can build,
  demolish, move ships and units, set missions and make peace and war.

Packages can also be zip archives, Ctrl+Shift+R reads them again in a running game, and the mods
screen turns on the packages a package needs and says why one is not loaded. The modding guide is
in [docs/modding](https://github.com/x4rd4s/open1602/tree/v0.5.0/docs/modding), with an example
package in `data/mods/example`.

### Fixes

- The fertility view shows nothing on an island you have neither explored nor settled on.
- A mine whose ore vein is exhausted tells you once, with message, voice and video, as in the
  original.
- Units a scenario puts aboard a ship are on board (the tutorial's land battle started with an
  empty ship).
- The status bar shows a rival's new city or ship with an exclamation mark in the rival's colour,
  as in the original.
- The computers' supply ships buy at the pirates' nest and at native villages again.
- A city without a standing market or Kontor holds 10 t of each ware, a ship at a destroyed Kontor
  offers trading where a trade treaty allows it, and taking a city over is paid with the material
  on a ship within reach, as in the original.
- Build menu: a click on another category while a pop-up is open opens it at once, the pop-up
  stands above the category buttons, and a right click deselects the category.
- Birds and surf at a close zoom on a large screen are heard only within the original's view, so a
  big forest no longer chirps several times a second.
- Dragging a volume slider no longer slows the game down; the settings are saved when it is let go.
- Ships find their sea routes about two and a half times faster.
