## What's new in 0.6.0

Changes since [0.5.0](https://github.com/x4rd4s/open1602/releases/tag/v0.5.0): Open1602 now updates
itself from its releases, the improved computer players plan, trade, make alliances and wage war,
the editor gains many tools, and there is a package for Linux on ARM.

Multiplayer games need every player on 0.6.0: the network protocol has changed. Games saved with
earlier versions still load.

### New

- **Updates:** the button "Updates" at the bottom right of the main menu shows the installed and
  the newest version, downloads and installs a new one and restarts the game. At the first start
  Open1602 asks once whether to check for new versions at every start; when one is out, the main
  menu offers "Later", "Install" or "Skip this version". The version before is kept and can be
  restored on the same screen. Savegames, settings and mods are never touched. Only the packages of
  a release update themselves; other copies get a link to the download page. This is the first
  version with the updater, so it has to be downloaded by hand once. Details in
  [docs/updates.md](https://github.com/x4rd4s/open1602/blob/v0.6.0/docs/updates.md).
- **Linux ARM64:** a package for 64-bit ARM Linux (`linux-arm64`).
- **Cities per island:** islands can hold up to 31 cities. The new setting "Cities per island" on the
  page of the balance changes offers 7 (as in the original), 12, 16 (the default), 24 or 31, so that
  more of twelve players can share an island.
- **Shortage icon in the top bar:** over an own city the top bar shows the icon of the good the city
  lacks most while its supply keeps falling, as in the original.
- **Island world:** sliders for the number of ore mountains, inland lakes and ore veins. Islands of
  their own shape lay dry land in patches as the climate's templates do: fallow land in the north, a
  desert heart of sand, cacti and steppe in the south.
- **Copy tool and blueprints** build what the material and gold reach, roads and fields first.
- **Spectators** of a multiplayer game can set the speed and read the city windows, and a game can be
  joined only to watch.

### Improved computer players

With "Computer players" not set to "As the original" the computers now play much more like a person:

- **No gifts:** they get no money or material from nowhere any more and live from what they earn.
  They set their taxes as high as each class stays content, spend their gold from one budget and
  work their way out of debt by tearing down unneeded producers or giving up their costliest city.
- **They see what a player sees:** they settle, search for crops and attack only on islands their
  ships have discovered, and send idle ships to explore.
- **Strategy and diplomacy:** each computer has a character (trader, conqueror or settler) and a
  stance towards every rival. Computers make peace with each other, players and computers make
  alliances in the diplomacy window, ask their allies for help and join their wars. They can wage
  war on any rival, a human or a computer, and offer peace when the war gets nowhere.
- **Army:** they arm by their inhabitants and their rivals' armies, build castles on their own and
  pay their army's upkeep from a war budget.
- **Towns:** they plan their towns in zones with a style of their own, with fields at the edge and
  public buildings among the houses, grow their home city only as its supply allows, plan the rise
  of their classes ahead and grow food before it runs out. When hungry and no site on a road is
  left, they plant a food farm off their roads. They place farms only where a market's carts reach
  them, and a market at the edge of their land claims open land for further farms.
- **Colonies and ships:** they choose their next colony among the original's motives, ranked by the
  city it becomes, with room for a main city, for the goods they run out of first or for gold and
  ore. They supply far cities with several ships, weigh the way at sea, keep their supply ships and
  colony goods moving, load their colony ships in one go and move up their colonies' Kontors. They
  keep their shipyard when they build the big one and build shipyards only where their carriers
  reach a store, so ship orders no longer get stuck. Damaged warships no longer crowd the home
  Kontor.

### Editor

- New tools: a fertility view, a pipette for buildings, painting territory, land up to the map's
  edge, trade routes for ships and carts from the start of the game, and units that patrol from the
  start.
- Place trade carts, objects without an owner (wild fields, ruins, unused buildings) and neutral
  buildings; natives and pirates can own cities and ships; any vein in any ore mountain, as in the
  original editor.
- Set what a Kontor or chief's hut sells and buys, how players start towards natives, pirates and
  the trader, and a lasting peace with the pirates. A page for music and sound volumes.
- Test play as any player; the test game ends back in the editor.
- New maps with random islands and random crops. Islands of older maps can be taken out once
  cleared. Buildings are shown in their turn and look before the click, houses in their random
  looks, and units and native huts by their names in the game's language.

### Fixes

- Loaded games go on exactly as the saved game would have, and computers keep arming for a war
  after loading.
- Exploring an island finds all its ore and gold deposits in one go, and native villages keep their
  fields and land on islands of their own shape.
- Large maps get more free traders, as many more as the world is larger.
- While building, the own coast is lit like the rest of the own land, as in the original.
- The diplomacy window shows with whom a partner is at war.
- A fallen town's land goes to the neighbouring towns, as in the original.
- Trade routes keep each ware in the holds its entries name, so part loads no longer mix up a
  ship's holds.
- Ships can sell to a computer's Kontor whatever its trade window shows as wanted.
- Soldiers follow their orders in large battles, landed soldiers no longer end up behind another
  player's wall, cavalry in player colours no longer shows specks, and the large merchant ship is
  faster.
- Withering fields go through their stages of withering, as in the original.
- Each sound plays at most once at a time, as in the History Edition.
- The window of the extended options is called "Settings", and the goods window reacts to every
  click.
