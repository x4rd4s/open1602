## What's new in 0.9.0

Changes since [0.8.0](https://github.com/x4rd4s/open1602/releases/tag/v0.8.0): the improved computer
players keep their economy together in debt and in short supply, wage war only where it gains them
land, land and arm better, and count their rivals' cities and soldiers only as seen; the editor can
draw start places at random and open a player's buildings up to a stage, and spectators see the
score sheets while the game runs and see every deposit and building radius.

Multiplayer games need every player on 0.9.0: the network protocol has changed. Games saved with
earlier versions still load.

### Improved computer players

With "Computer players" not set to "As the original":

- **What they know:** below the hardest level, a computer counts the cities and soldiers of the
  others only as far as it has seen them, as a player does on the map: when it weighs a trade
  offer, searches for an island to settle and judges a rival's army. The hardest level sees as
  before.
- **Debt:** a computer in debt keeps standing what has sold within the last hour, and the raw
  materials for it, and switches farms with fields off instead of tearing them down.
- **Luxury goods:** when no free island grows a crop fully, no partner offers its good and a city
  has run dry of it, a computer plants it on half-fertile land, if it can pay for it now.
- **Wars:** a computer that lacks a luxury crop turns its war plan on a rival holding an island
  that grows it, but only for a crop its own islands do not grow and a city has run dry of. A war
  starts only below its city limit and with a colony its budget accepts, so that it can settle
  what it razes, and ends once the razing gains nothing more. A shortfall its stock covers no
  longer holds a war back.
- **Cities:** a computer settles an island its own war freed, even above its city limit, and goes
  beyond the limit otherwise only as far as the game's victory asks for more cities. Small islands
  with a free gold vein are taken for the gold.
- **Landings:** soldiers land on the target's island, not on an islet in front of it, and take on
  the towers beside any market they attack first. A ship with soldiers aboard and nothing to do
  takes them home before any supply run. A computer lands on an island it chose beside a small
  rival's city, and the island search (the pirates' too) passes over islands it would turn back
  from instead of sailing there again and again.
- **Arming:** a war party too small for a city's towers is raised for that city, several units at
  once and beyond the army budget while the gold pays its upkeep. A breach takes a wall on any
  coast, not only on a beach near the market. A computer outmatched by a near rival it is not at
  peace with plans its first castle.
- **Growth:** citizens move on to the next stage with an income instead of waiting for a large gold
  reserve, and a stage is left on gold alone only once its own buildings are there and the
  citizens have begun to come.
- **Doctors:** the next doctor is built where houses lack one, and where there is no site left, a
  house makes way for it in the place that reaches the most houses.
- **Ships:** a ship under the white flag keeps it until the pirates have gone. A shipyard pauses
  its building while a damaged ship is called in, and a repair starts only with the material in the
  yard.
- **Trade:** a computer books at a partner's Kontor only what its runs can still load there;
  below the hardest level it counts only its own runs, as a player would.

### Editor

- **Start places at random** (page Description): the players who start with ships but without a
  city draw their start places when the game starts, among the places of their first ships and the
  point markers made a ship start place ("Make ship start place").
- **First island** (page Players, tab "Computers", was "Attitude"): per computer as the original,
  at random, or a fixed island of the map, which it looks at first for its first city. The tab
  explains its columns in tooltips.
- **Buildings open from the start** (page Players): a player's build menu can be open from the
  start up to the pioneers' to the aristocrats' buildings.
- The first four players can be removed too, as long as they own nothing.
- The page Cargo shows the holds a ship has in a game of the map, by the map's rules.
- The owner list and the ship's properties name the free trader instead of "Player 7".
- Room for longer player names, and texts that fit in English and German.

### Multiplayer and spectators

- In a multiplayer game and for spectators, the rating shows every player's score sheet while the
  game goes on, refreshed every second; a spectator starts with the leader's. Alone, the rating
  still stops the game.
- Spectators hear of every player's city growing and of every war and peace, and see the crops of
  every island.
- Spectators see every ore and gold deposit and the full status line over every island, whatever
  the world's settings, and the radius of any player's building they click. Over a player's city,
  the top line shows that player's gold and building materials.

### Interface

- The main menu shows the version beside the button Updates.
- The Finances tab of the statistics shows the gold in the treasury and, when the balance is
  negative, how many minutes it lasts.
- The lowest step of the volume sliders is silent, in the game and in the editor.
- The Kontor's demand button, which did nothing, is hidden.
- No more one-pixel gap between the main menu's buttons at some window sizes.

### Fixes

- A road, house or field demolished beside the beach no longer leaves a strip of open water in the
  island.
- With "Barren patches: As the original", the sand floor among the meadows counts as poor ground
  by its picture (bare sand a quarter, sand fraying into grass a half or three quarters) instead of
  carrying crops in full, and the fertility views show it so.

### Documentation

- A shorter README that opens with what Open1602 is; installing and playing moved to
  docs/install.md and docs/playing.md. The status page is up to date.
