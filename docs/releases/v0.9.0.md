## What's new in 0.9.0

Changes since 0.5.0: computer opponents that play as equal rivals, packages for Linux on ARM64, a
richer editor and many fixes from players' feedback.

Multiplayer games need every player on 0.9.0: the network protocol has changed. Games saved with
earlier versions still load.

### Computer opponents

With "Computer players" set to "Improved" (the default; "As the original" keeps the original's), the
computers now play by the players' rules and against every rival, not only against the humans:

- **No gifts:** they get no money, building material, subsidies or shortened droughts from nowhere.
  They set their taxes as a player does, move on to the next stage with less gold, and hold back
  their building material while they are in debt.
- **The same view and the same units:** they found towns, look for crops and attack only on islands
  their ships have discovered, send idle ships exploring, and their soldiers and ships attack on
  orders, as a player's do.
- **A character of their own:** each draws one per game (trader, conqueror or settler) and weighs
  every rival, human or computer, for its goal (economy, military, gold or land). On harder levels
  they look at their rivals more often; on the easiest they never start a war against a human.
- **Diplomacy among all players:** they trade with each other, make peace with each other, offer
  and accept alliances, ask their allies for help and join their wars. Players can make alliances
  with them in the diplomacy window too.
- **War against any rival:** a computer that presses a weaker, disliked neighbour declares war,
  arms, lands troops and takes the rival's cities, a computer's as well as a human's. It checks the
  war's progress and offers peace when it stops paying off.
- **Land and expansion:** they found colonies for what their goal needs, contest small rivals'
  islands for gold and ore, and settle more than four islands.
- **Economy and towns:** their armies grow with their inhabitants and rivals, and they build castles
  on their own. They plan their towns in zones in a style of their own, with fields at the edge and
  public buildings amid the houses, fit their production to what their cities need, weigh the way
  at sea when they fetch goods, and no longer get stuck at a few houses or on a site they cannot
  build on.
- **War economy:** they pay their army's upkeep as players do and arm only within a budget from
  what their cities earn: less in peace, more while arming, at war or under threat. A core army
  defends them always, and a city without ore on its islands looks for an ore colony.
- **Supply and growth:** they supply far cities, keep gold for their purchases, grow the home city
  with its supply, load their colony ships, claim land for their farms, secure their alcohol, let
  their Kontors face every side and keep their shipyard while they build the big one.
  They plan the rise of their classes ahead and keep their supply ships and colony goods moving.
- **Out of debt:** a computer in debt tears down producers it does not need, gives up its costliest
  city and founds no colony without income, so it no longer stays in debt for good.

### New

- **Linux on ARM64:** a package for 64-bit ARM computers (`linux-arm64`).
- **Editor:** test play as any player, and back to the editor after a lost test game; place trade carts, neutral buildings that soldiers can take,
  special objects without owner (wild fields, ruins, unused buildings); set what a Kontor or chief's
  hut sells and buys, how players start towards natives, pirates and the trader, and a lasting
  peace with the pirates; natives and pirates can own cities; a page for music and sound volumes;
  buildings shown with their house classes and looks, and named native huts; a fertility view
  (Ctrl+B), a pipette that takes buildings, painting a city's territory without a building
  (Shift+G), trade routes for placed ships and trade carts that run from the start, and land up to
  the map's edge without a coast.
- **Editor:** a big iron vein can go into a small ore mountain, as in the original editor; in the
  game the ore mine digs it down to 120 t and a deep mine the rest.
- **Island status line:** also lists the crops an island grows beyond its climate's.
- **New maps** with random islands and random crops.
- **Island world:** sliders for the number of ore veins, ore mountains and inland lakes; dry land in
  patches on islands of their own shape; up to 31 cities per island (setting "Cities per island").
- **Spectators:** a "Watch" button in the game search and the address panel joins a game only to
  watch; spectators read every city's windows and, with the new lobby setting "Spectators: speed",
  set the game speed for everyone.
- **Watchtowers** can no longer be placed next to an enemy soldier; the original's way is the new
  original setting "Towers near enemies".
- **Trade routes** keep each ware in the holds its entries name, so part loads never push one ware
  into another's hold.
- **Free trader for a single Kontor:** a world with only one Kontor gets visits from the free
  trader too; between visits he sails out to the map edge and back.
- **Top bar:** the icon of the good an own city lacks most, as in the original.
- **Blueprints and the copy tool** build what the means reach, roads and fields first.
- **Large battles:** soldiers follow orders in large crowds, walls stop landings and cannon balls,
  and the large merchant ship is faster.

### Fixes

- Each sound plays at most once at a time.
- Exploring uncovers whole islands, native villages keep their land, and large maps have more
  traders.
- Withered fields wither through their pictures, as in the original.
- A fallen town's land is taken as in the original.
- Ships can sell to a computer's Kontor whatever its trade window asks for.
- The goods window reacts to every click, and its balance line is clickable on all its letters.
- The options window is called "Settings".
