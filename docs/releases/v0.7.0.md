## What's new in 0.7.0

Changes since [0.6.0](https://github.com/x4rd4s/open1602/releases/tag/v0.6.0): the improved computer
players wage war much better, soldiers walk smoothly in large games, spectators see far more of a
multiplayer game, and the editor gains new options for players, ships and campaigns.

Multiplayer games need every player on 0.7.0: the network protocol has changed. Games saved with
earlier versions still load.

### Improved computer players

With "Computer players" not set to "As the original":

- **They judge their rivals by what a player sees:** the forces, warships and cannons of other
  players count only as far as their ships have seen them, and a rival counts as near and as a
  target only by the cities they know of. Below the hardest level they know a partner's Kontor only
  as a player would.
- **War:** a computer goes to war when its income carries it, not only on a large gold reserve, and
  keeps pressing a rival until the rival likes it again. Warships and trade ships are told apart by
  type, and warships take on their cannons at a Kontor.
- **Landings:** troop ships sail on to land their soldiers instead of chasing other ships, and move
  to where the soldiers waiting at the Kontor can board. Soldiers land where they can walk into the
  target city. Against a coast walled all along, or a free beach sealed off by trees or a wall, a
  computer shoots a gap to land.
- **Defence:** a castle whose way out is cut off keeps its units, and the guard no longer calls back
  soldiers that are already on their way aboard a ship.
- **Building:** mines only where a Kontor's or market's carts reach them, quarries where a
  stonemason's circle holds them, no waiting for carts at a quarry, and crops counted as their own
  only where their farm finds a site. Producers whose good fills the warehouse stay switched off
  instead of being torn down and built anew.

### Multiplayer and spectators

- Spectators read the holds of every ship and trade cart, see every player's ships in the ship list
  with the owner's coat of arms, and can look into castles and shipyards without giving orders.
- In the goods balance (F2) a spectator chooses whose figures to read: a click on the title goes
  through the players.
- A spectator sees a city's inhabitants under the mouse, as in the city list.
- When the game starts together, ships no longer start on or against a coast.

### Editor

- Players: exact starting money (also a debt), and a tab "Attitude" that sets when a computer grows
  cross with a player.
- Description: "Player picks the flag" shows the colour screen before the scenario starts.
- Ships and carts: names, land units and cannons on board, ships offered for sale from the start,
  and trade carts on any road of their owner's land.
- Neutral buildings can be made peaceful: at peace with everyone, and they go over to a city whose
  land comes to cover them.
- Paint territory can take land back with the left button too.
- Maps of one name with a trailing digit appear in the main menu as one campaign with parts, and
  a package campaign can open all of its parts from the start.
- Tooltips of the wide dialogs stay on the screen.

### Gameplay and interface

- Soldiers walk on smoothly in games with many units instead of stopping every few steps, find their
  way to their goal faster and no longer crowd around the spot they were sent to.
- Soldiers on an attack move against a building fight back an enemy unit within reach first, and
  then go on with the building.
- The window of someone else's unit or ship shows the owner's coat of arms and name.
- A right click on any page of a ship's window (exchange of goods, trading at a foreign Kontor, the
  pirates' pages) goes back to the ship's own page.
- The build preview hides the trees and crops under a dragged field.
- The house window fits a five-digit inhabitant count, and the question dialog wraps long lines.

### Fixes

- After a building is demolished, the ground comes back as meadow instead of water with dark edges.
- An alliance offer made again after a withdrawal is renewed instead of piling up goodwill.
- A computer's trade cart drives only to partners it is at peace with.
- The pirates' nest keeps what the map set it to buy.
