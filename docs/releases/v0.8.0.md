## What's new in 0.8.0

Changes since [0.7.0](https://github.com/x4rd4s/open1602/releases/tag/v0.7.0): the improved computer
players attack from the sea much more like a player, with convoys and towers shelled or stormed
before a landing, text fields get a caret and the clipboard, and
the editor and content packages gain new options.

Multiplayer games need every player on 0.8.0: the network protocol has changed. Games saved with
earlier versions still load.

### Improved computer players

With "Computer players" not set to "As the original":

- **Towers:** a city whose markets and Kontor are gone but whose towers still stand is no longer
  left alone: the soldiers go for its towers, the nearest first, and cut their way through a forest
  around one.
- **Landings under towers:** towers that cover a landing beach or the way inland are shelled first
  by as few free warships as can do it without losing one, when that pays; the troops wait until
  then. Where no fleet can wreck such towers, a landing party storms them only when it is big
  enough, and otherwise the city is passed over instead of landing soldiers under the towers' fire.
- **Convoys:** a party too big for one ship sails in a convoy of troop ships, which unload together
  off the beach and land together or not at all; a ship that arrives late joins the soldiers
  already ashore. A convoy counts the soldiers already aboard and may take an empty armed supply
  ship off its route, but not while a city waits for what that route brings.
- **Attacks that can succeed:** an attack starts only where the landing will go ahead, so ships are
  no longer sent back and forth without landing anyone. A troop ship waits for the soldiers still
  walking aboard, lies where they can reach it, and a ship with room for three soldiers or fewer
  no longer attacks alone.
- **On land:** soldiers take on the towers over their way to the market or Kontor first; all of
  them cut the trees in the way to a tower, and a soldier whose target is gone takes the next
  order or stops.
- **Ships:** a badly damaged ship goes to the yard instead of being sent to an attack again.
- **Debt:** a computer in debt recruits no soldiers and builds no towers.
- **Mines:** a mine is built where the carts of its city reach it, and elsewhere only when no such
  placement can be built. A city whose only mine of a kind lies out of its carts' reach builds one
  market towards it.

### Text fields

- Every text field, including the names of ships, cities, savegames and the player, has a caret:
  the arrows, Home and End move it, Shift selects, Ctrl+A selects everything, Delete removes the
  letter after it, and Ctrl+C, Ctrl+X and Ctrl+V copy, cut and paste through the system's
  clipboard.
- A word that ends in a hyphen keeps the space after it when the text wraps.

### Multiplayer and spectators

- Spectators see the owner's coat of arms in the heading of every ship, unit and trade cart, and
  the unit identification shows the coat of arms over every player's land units.
- The scenario list of the multiplayer menu holds the scenarios of content packages; each part of
  a campaign is an entry of its own.

### Editor

- A map of open sea gets the free trader with its first player, as a new game does; a map saved
  without him gets him when it is opened in the editor.
- Ships for sale can have a price of their own, and the pirates' and natives' ships can be offered
  for sale too (players buy them only at peace with the seller).
- Players can be given the natives' spearmen.
- The pirates can have a trade cart at their nest that trades with the cities of its island at
  peace with them.

### Gameplay and interface

- A trade cart's window shows the coat of arms of someone else's cart in its heading, as the ship's
  window does.
- A destroyed market collapses after a while, as in the original.
- When a native village or a computer's given-up city is dissolved, its land within another city's
  market, Kontor or tower range goes to that city at once (not with the original setting for the
  land of razed villages).
- In the main menu a scenario that is part of a package's campaign is chosen inside the campaign
  and no longer listed again on its own.

### Content packages

- Music and sounds in Microsoft or IMA ADPCM WAVE files, 24/32-bit PCM and float play; a file the
  game cannot play is reported when the package is loaded, and a track that fails is skipped
  instead of being tried again and again.

### Fixes

- Soldiers now also board their own ship when it lies 4 to 4½ tiles off the coast; the order was
  dropped without a word before (a fault of the original game).
